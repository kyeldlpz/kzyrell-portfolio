## C. Recommendations

On the basis of the foregoing findings and conclusions, the researchers formulate the following recommendations intended to strengthen the system's technical performance, support its responsible adoption in elderly care institutions, and inform future research directions. The researchers arrived with the following:

**1. Multi-Camera or Depth-Sensing Augmentation to Mitigate Foreshortening.** The three false negatives observed during fall detection were traced back to visual foreshortening — a well-known limitation inherent to 2D monocular vision, where a person falling directly toward or away from the camera causes their body to compress in the image, making it difficult for the pose estimator to distinguish the fall from normal posture. Because this is a constraint of the camera geometry rather than a shortcoming of the AI model, no amount of model refinement alone can fully resolve it. To address this, future research should explore the integration of multiple cameras positioned at complementary angles within each monitoring zone, so that if one viewpoint loses visibility of a fall direction, another can compensate. An alternative or supplementary approach involves depth-sensing hardware — such as stereo camera pairs or time-of-flight sensors compatible with edge computing platforms — which would provide the system with three-dimensional spatial information and eliminate the ambiguity that foreshortening introduces.

**2. Expansion and Diversification of Training Datasets.** The training data used for gait analysis in this study comprised controlled video samples of individuals walking independently, categorized as either Normal or Abnormal. While this proved effective under controlled evaluation conditions, real-world deployment at the Magokoro Elderly Facility revealed that movement in an actual care setting is far more varied and unpredictable than any laboratory simulation can capture. Future studies should curate datasets that include a wider range of real-world movement scenarios typically observed within Filipino elderly care facilities — such as assisted walking with a caregiver, use of mobility aids including walkers and wheelchairs, transitions from seated to standing positions, and navigation through socially active or crowded common areas. In addition, the fall detection training data should be expanded to include samples captured from diverse camera angles and under varied lighting conditions, so that the model develops greater robustness to the environmental variability it will inevitably encounter during deployment across different facilities and room configurations.

**3. Integration of Non-Intrusive Fall Prevention Mechanisms.** The CAIretaker system, as currently designed, functions primarily as a monitoring and alert mechanism — it detects falls and abnormal gait patterns and notifies caregivers accordingly, but it does not possess the capability to prevent a fall from occurring in the first place. Future research and development should explore the integration of active fall prevention mechanisms that extend the system's role from reactive detection and proactive alerting to genuine preventive intervention. Critically, any such prevention mechanism must not require the elderly resident to wear, carry, or actively interact with any device. The research literature consistently demonstrates that wearable-based prevention solutions — including airbag vests, sensor bracelets, and instrumented garments — tend to fail in practice because elderly users find them uncomfortable, forget to wear them, or actively resist using them altogether (Lu & Chu, 2018; Tamura et al., 2009; Nemeth et al., 2022). Any proposed prevention mechanism should therefore be embedded entirely within the care environment, operating autonomously and imposing zero additional burden on the resident.

Several practical directions merit exploration: (a) automated environmental responses triggered when the system detects abnormal gait — such as activating pathway lighting, engaging wheelchair brakes, or issuing localized audio cues to alert nearby staff, all without requiring any action from the resident; (b) smart furniture systems capable of adjusting bed height, raising bed rails, or securing reclining chairs when the system senses a resident attempting to stand unassisted; (c) conversion of gait analysis data into personalized risk scores compiled into daily or weekly reports for caregivers, enabling targeted preventive action such as physical therapy referrals, mobility aid adjustments, or environmental modifications around high-risk individuals; and (d) a tiered alert framework that distinguishes between confirmed falls requiring immediate emergency response and elevated risk states warranting preventive check-ins, allowing caregivers to prioritize their attention based on urgency.

The financial dimension also warrants careful consideration. Most elderly care facilities in the Philippines — whether government-operated or institutionally partnered — function under constrained budgets. Smart furniture and automated environmental response systems available on the international market can carry significant costs. Future research should prioritize the identification of affordable, locally available, and modular components that facilities can adopt incrementally without requiring a prohibitive upfront investment. Cost-benefit analyses tailored to Philippine facility budgets should be undertaken to determine the threshold at which the cost of prevention technology is justified relative to the ongoing healthcare expenditures associated with fall-related injuries.

**4. Hardware Upgrade to Enhance System Performance.** The Raspberry Pi 5 paired with the Hailo AI HAT+ accelerator delivered functional real-time performance throughout the evaluation period. However, the scalability tests revealed that when ten or more individuals appeared in the camera frame simultaneously, inference times increased by as much as 36.44%. While the system remained operational and never became unusable, there is a clear ceiling to what the current hardware platform can sustain — a limitation that becomes increasingly relevant if the system is to be deployed in larger facilities with higher resident occupancy. Future implementations should explore transitioning to more powerful edge computing platforms, such as the NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano or Jetson Orin NX, which offer substantially higher neural processing throughput compared to the Raspberry Pi architecture. Complementary hardware upgrades — including cameras with higher resolution and improved low-light sensitivity, as well as active cooling solutions — would further support the system's detection accuracy and hardware longevity during continuous, round-the-clock operation.
